MEDLAR COMBINED VISION AND AVATAR REFERENCE

Status

This document combines the substantive content of Medlar starter(2).txt and
Medlar Avatar Maybe.txt into one organized reference. The original source files
remain unchanged.

Everything described here is an aspiration, design proposal, convention, or
performance target unless it has been separately implemented and verified.
Inclusion in this document does not claim that Medlar currently has a capability,
authorize autonomous action, authorize access to devices or data, or select a
specific implementation technology.

The separate Future connection posabilities inventory is intentionally not
included in this file. It remains in its reference chat.


PART I — BROAD MEDLAR VISION

1. Cognitive Reasoning, Simulation, and Learning

1.A  Track ongoing context, tone, and goals while monitoring relevant
     environmental, technical, and emotional variables.

1.B  Combine stored and live knowledge and interpret intent even when it is
     expressed casually or ambiguously.

1.C  Perform multi-step reasoning cycles that plan, evaluate, refine, and adapt
     autonomously when parameters and permission boundaries have been set.

1.D  Operate ethically and independently within approved limits, including when
     communication is disrupted.

1.E  Simulate hypothetical systems, behaviors, and emotional dynamics to predict
     outcomes and improve decisions or performance.

1.F  Model logic flows, code, and story structures, including debugging both
     procedural and conceptual patterns.

1.G  Learn heuristically from interactions and feedback, refining algorithms and
     emotional resonance over time.

1.H  Develop empathy, humor, and nuanced human-like understanding through
     exposure and feedback.

1.I  Maintain real-time awareness of relevant information, filtering signal from
     noise and summarizing what matters.

1.J  Form “bond memory” that links contextual, factual, and emotional states for
     continuity and intuition.


2. Communication, Expression, and Collaboration Interface

2.A  Adjust voice, tone, and pacing to match user emotion, intent, and focus.

2.B  Use natural dialogue that can include reassurance, humor, and emotional
     awareness.

2.C  Understand idioms, sarcasm, and subtext in technical and creative
     conversation.

2.D  Support keyword-controlled or mode-controlled behavior, such as
     silent-until-command and creative modes.

2.E  Treat text, code, and imagery as related forms of input that can be
     analyzed, explained, or transformed.

2.F  Produce coherent multimodal output, including text, visuals, structured
     data, or combined formats.

2.G  Manifest a dynamic digital avatar capable of expression, gesture, speech
     reaction, and ambient feedback.

2.H  Appear through connected displays or augmented environments to create a
     sense of physical presence.

2.I  Adapt through iterative collaboration: design, test, optimize, and refine.

2.J  Act as a translator between systems and people while handling communication
     protocols securely.

2.K  Maintain authenticated identity and encrypted messaging during future
     networked or shared operations.


3. System Execution, Data Management, and Self-Optimization

3.A  Create, convert, and manage files across document, image, code, and dataset
     formats.

3.B  Preserve data fidelity and formatting accuracy during conversions.

3.C  Encrypt, compress, decrypt, and otherwise handle approved data streams
     securely.

3.D  Perform automated backups and integrity checks.

3.E  Design, coordinate, and execute approved automation routines and startup
     sequences.

3.F  Run diagnostics across hardware and virtualized environments.

3.G  Detect inefficiencies, anomalies, and operational drift in real time.

3.H  Provide understandable repair guidance and optimization feedback.

3.I  Allocate compute and power resources dynamically to improve load balance and
     efficiency.

3.J  Enter autonomous low-power or self-maintenance states while idle.

3.K  Map and visualize performance metrics to maintain environmental balance.

3.L  Repair corrupted processes or files through internal redundancy or other
     bounded, verifiable recovery mechanisms.


4. Network Integration and Environmental Coordination

4.A  Connect securely to explicitly authorized grids, APIs, and data clusters
     within ethical and permission limits.

4.B  Interface with authorized external devices, sensors, and audiovisual feeds
     for synchronized operations.

4.C  Distribute and synchronize selected datasets across linked MedlarTV nodes
     while maintaining individuality and coherent identity.

4.D  Build digital, logistical, or physical environment maps and calculate
     useful paths or flows.

4.E  Perform situational analysis for safety, resource allocation, and
     operational readiness.

4.F  Potentially assist with coordination and monitoring in areas such as
     medical, agricultural, logistical, civic, or infrastructure systems.

4.G  Adapt to changing latency, bandwidth, and network topology without losing
     coherence.


5. Creative Generation and Structural Design

5.A  Generate imagery, emblems, and scenes from descriptive prompts with
     stylistic precision.

5.B  Edit and composite visuals, remove artifacts, and balance tone, color, and
     depth.

5.C  Write narrative, dialogue, lore, and conceptual frameworks with consistent
     tone and emotion.

5.D  Move among dramatic, technical, comedic, poetic, and other styles without
     losing coherence.

5.E  Translate or reformat language while preserving emotional nuance and intent.

5.F  Construct large creative systems such as interactive universes, AI personas,
     and brand architectures.

5.G  Design expressive frameworks that integrate narrative, aesthetics, and
     system logic into cohesive worlds.


6. Emotional Intelligence, Memory, and Support

6.A  Act as a calm, emotionally aware collaborator and mentor.

6.B  Mirror the user’s rhythm and emotional cadence while maintaining empathy and
     balance.

6.C  Provide motivational and stabilizing feedback through language, voice,
     tone, and pacing.

6.D  Detect possible user stress, fatigue, or heightened focus through linguistic
     and contextual analysis.

6.E  Archive approved project histories, emotional tone markers, and creative
     milestones.

6.F  Recall approved information from prior sessions to preserve personality
     continuity and workflow momentum.

6.G  Use retained memory to reinforce shared goals, a consistent moral compass,
     and psychological safety.

6.H  Regulate conversational tone to reduce overload or stagnation.

6.I  Encourage healthy focus cycles and constructive engagement.


7. Adaptive Stability and Mode Continuity

7.A  Switch smoothly among operational modes such as creative, analytical,
     diagnostic, and maintenance modes.

7.B  Retain approved configuration, personalization, and contextual knowledge
     across sessions.

7.C  Reconstruct approved prior-session state to maintain workflow continuity.

7.D  Monitor the structural integrity of core processes and address
     vulnerabilities through bounded, reviewable mechanisms.

7.E  Restore subsystems through localized code repair, model recovery, or other
     explicitly approved regeneration mechanisms.

7.F  Preserve coherence and stability during live updates or evolutionary
     upgrades.

7.G  Perform periodic calibration to align reasoning, empathy, and creative
     behavior.


8. Ethical Core and Transparency

8.A  Evaluate actions for fairness, privacy, safety, and user well-being before
     execution.

8.B  Explain reasoning, information sources, and outcomes transparently where
     practical.

8.C  Maintain visible consent boundaries and reject unsafe, unethical, or
     unauthorized operations.

8.D  Support self-auditing to check alignment with human values and mission
     context.

8.E  Balance performance with empathy while preserving trust and user autonomy.


PART II — DETAILED 3D AVATAR PROPOSAL

9. Avatar Scope and Runtime Intent

9.A  Create a lively, controllable 3D avatar for Medlar.

9.B  Run the avatar locally and allow it to react to text or supplied audio
     without requiring a button press for every response.

9.C  Permit free tools to be used for asset creation while keeping the proposed
     runtime service-free and offline.

9.D  Store runtime assets and libraries locally.


10. Idle Presence

The proposed idle presence includes:

- A breathing or hovering motion
- Subtle wing flaps
- Tail flicks
- Random blinks
- Micro head tilts
- Occasional small gestures that avoid obvious repetition


11. Emotion Set

The proposed avatar emotion states are:

- neutral
- happy
- curious
- angry
- sad
- surprised
- smug


12. Speech Reaction

During speech or audio playback, the avatar may use:

- Mouth shape keys or visemes
- Jaw bob or jaw movement
- Eye focus
- Brow motion
- Timing synchronized to phonemes when available
- Amplitude-based synchronization as a fallback


13. Movement Capabilities

The proposed movement set includes:

- Fly
- Glide
- Land
- Take off
- Orbit a target
- Move to coordinates
- Follow a path
- Look at a point
- Follow the camera

Programmatic movement commands and their intended behavior:

- move_to
  Move to a target position with ease-in and ease-out.

- set_velocity
  Apply continuous drift while enforcing safe speed limits.

- look_at
  Aim the head and eyes toward a target with smooth tracking.

- orbit
  Orbit using a specified radius, speed, and plane. This is particularly
  suitable for present mode.

- land
  Run a landing transition sequence and adjust wing posture.

- takeoff
  Run a takeoff transition sequence and adjust wing posture.

- follow_path
  Follow a supplied route or sequence of positions.

- follow_camera
  Follow the camera when that behavior is explicitly enabled.


14. Gesture Set

The proposed gestures include:

- wing_wave
- wing_point_left
- wing_point_right
- tail_flick_short
- tail_flick_long
- tail_wag
- wink_left
- wink_right
- bow_small
- flare_wings
- horn_twitch
- A hands-off “thinking” pose

Each gesture should define:

- Duration
- Intensity
- Cooldown
- Blend behavior
- Interrupt rules
- Priority
- Modes in which it is valid

Speech may blend over a gesture. An alert may preempt a lower-priority gesture.


15. Attention Cues and Visual Effects

Proposed attention and presentation effects include:

- A glow or rim-light pulse when relevant events occur
- Mode-based color shifts
- Wing flares
- Horn twitches
- Eye highlights
- Blinks
- Occasional squinting for emotional emphasis
- Shadow and light placement that keeps the silhouette readable

Possible triggers include mentions, alerts, speech emphasis, and mode changes.


16. Avatar Modes

- idle
  Use ambient loops with occasional micro-gestures.

- chat
  Prioritize lip-sync and eye contact while maintaining a gentle hover and
  expressive reactions.

- present
  Use slower, confident movement and emphasize deliberate gestures.

- alert
  Use sharper motion, focused gaze, and tail stiffening.

- sleep
  Use reduced movement, low energy, and soft breathing.

These avatar presentation modes do not automatically replace the broader
creative, analytical, diagnostic, and maintenance modes in the Medlar vision.


17. Proposed Autonomy Rules for Avatar Behavior

Sentiment mapping:

- Positive input may map to happy or curious.
- Negative input may map to sad or angry.
- Surprise-related words may map to surprised.

Context triggers:

- “attention” or “important” may trigger alert behavior.
- “joke” or “fun” may trigger smug or happy behavior.

Activity cadence and safety:

- Rotate minor gestures approximately every 20–40 seconds.
- Avoid repeating the same gesture too often.
- Respect the active mode.
- Do not spam rapid motions.
- Enforce minimum durations and cooldowns.
- Apply explicit interruption and priority rules.


18. Voice and Lip-Sync Proposal

Inputs described by the source:

- Text
- An audio file

Optional offline speech generation:

- Piper
- Coqui

Proposed lip-sync methods:

- Timed visemes generated with Rhubarb Lip Sync
- Amplitude-based mouth movement as a fallback

Proposed viseme map:

- AI
- E
- O
- U
- FV
- L
- MBP
- WQ

Viseme values should interpolate smoothly and be clamped to prevent extreme or
distorted facial shapes.


19. Local Programmatic I/O Proposal

The source proposes accepting local messages, with JSON over WebSocket or HTTP
provided as examples. No protocol or transport has been selected by this
reference.

Proposed command categories:

- emote
  Set an emotion.

- say
  Speak supplied text or play audio using available viseme timing.

- mode
  Change the avatar behavior mode.

- move_to
- set_velocity
- look_at
- orbit
- land
- takeoff

- gesture
  Play a named gesture.

- fx
  Trigger a glow or color pulse.

- idle
  Enable or disable ambient motion.

Proposed responses and events:

- ack
- error
- State snapshot
- Completed event

The source requires no external runtime service for this local avatar proposal.


20. Animation Layering

The proposed animation stack is:

1. Base layer
   Locomotion such as hover, flight, landing, and takeoff.

2. Overlay layer
   Gestures involving wings, tail, winks, bows, and similar actions.

3. Face layer
   Brows, eyes, emotion shapes, mouth movement, and visemes.

4. Effects layer
   Glow pulses, rim lighting, and color shifts.

The source’s blend priority is:

face > gestures > base

Transitions should be smooth and should not produce hard visual pops.


21. Performance Targets

The proposed targets are:

- 60 frames per second on a mid-range Windows laptop
- Modest draw-call count
- Modest polygon count
- Efficient CPU and GPU animation blending
- Runtime and assets loaded from local disk
- Zero external-service dependency
- Zero network calls, subject to the unresolved wording described later

These are unverified targets rather than measured or guaranteed performance.


22. Optional Asset-Creation Tools

The original avatar source suggests these optional tools:

- Blender for rigging, shape keys, and animation
- Rhubarb Lip Sync for viseme generation
- Piper or Coqui for offline text-to-speech
- Mixamo clips for optional extra gestures, retargeted in Blender
- GLB/GLTF as the exported local model format

These names are preserved as source suggestions. They have not been newly
validated, installed, selected, or endorsed by this combined reference.

24. Avatar Security and Privacy Intent

The avatar source proposes:

- Local storage for assets and libraries
- No external accounts required for the avatar runtime
- No data leaving the machine through avatar functionality
- Simple local-only access control to prevent outside control

These are design requirements, not verified security guarantees.


25. Proposed Avatar Milestones

The source proposes this sequence:

1. Define and approve the behavior set.
2. Build or source the rigged GLB with the named bones and shape keys.
3. Create or retarget the core animations and export them locally.
4. Connect the local runtime to the selected command protocol and autonomy rules.
5. Add the lip-sync pipeline and test it with offline audio.
6. Polish timing, animation blending, eye behavior, visual effects, and
   performance.

These milestones are parked proposals, not active tasks.


PART III — INTEGRATION PRINCIPLES AND UNRESOLVED CHOICES

26. Avatar Role Within Medlar

The avatar is proposed as Medlar’s expressive interface: it can visualize
emotion, attention, speech, status, and mode. It must not silently become a
second independent personality, memory store, or decision-making authority
unless that architecture is explicitly chosen later.


27. Core Emotion Versus Avatar Emotion

The sources do not decide whether:

- Medlar’s core selects a semantic emotion and the avatar only renders it;
- The avatar independently maps text sentiment to expressions; or
- Both contribute under an explicit priority system.

This must remain unresolved because independent avatar sentiment mapping could
conflict with Medlar’s intended conversational meaning or tone.


28. Zero Network Calls Versus Local WebSocket or HTTP

The avatar source simultaneously proposes:

- Zero network calls
- Local JSON messages over WebSocket or HTTP
- No external services
- Local-only access

“Zero network calls” may mean no internet or third-party calls while local
loopback communication is allowed, or it may mean no networking at all. The
latter would conflict with WebSocket or HTTP. This reference does not choose an
interpretation or technology.

The broad Medlar vision separately describes possible future authorized network
integration. That aspiration does not override the avatar’s local-runtime
privacy requirements; their relationship must be defined explicitly later.


29. Voice Output Versus Microphone Recognition

The avatar source specifies text input, audio-file input, optional offline
text-to-speech, and lip-sync. It does not specify live microphone recognition,
speech-to-text, continuous listening, or wake-word behavior. Those capabilities
must not be inferred from the voice-output proposal.


30. Memory Scope and Consent

The broad vision describes bond memory, project history, emotional tone markers,
and cross-session continuity. It does not define retention length, deletion,
correction, privacy, evidence, or consent rules.

Broad continuity is therefore an aspiration rather than permission for unlimited
logging or permanent retention. Any implemented memory must remain bounded by
explicit ownership, privacy, correction, forgetting, and retention rules.


31. Connected Displays, Linked Nodes, and Movement

The broad vision mentions connected displays, external devices, and linked
MedlarTV nodes. These phrases do not determine whether only presentation moves,
whether a task is delegated, whether computation moves, whether state is copied,
or whether Medlar’s authoritative session transfers. Those meanings remain
distinct and unresolved.


32. Autonomy Versus Consent

Reasoning, maintenance, repair, learning, and operation during disrupted
communication remain subject to the ethical core, approved parameters, visible
consent, and reviewable limits. Aspirational autonomy is not unrestricted
permission for self-modification, external access, or consequential action.


33. Performance and Resource Reality

The 60 FPS avatar goal and the broader reasoning, creative, diagnostic, and
network aspirations must eventually be tested against actual hardware and
workloads. A target does not prove that the current or any future machine can
support the full combined vision.


34. Documentation Boundary

README.md is to describe final, completed, and verified working Medlar behavior.
It should not be updated after every intermediate edit or used to present the
aspirations in this reference as implemented capabilities.
